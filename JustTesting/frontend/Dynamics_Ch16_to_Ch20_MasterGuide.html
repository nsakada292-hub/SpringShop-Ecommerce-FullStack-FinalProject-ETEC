CHAPTER 16 — Planar Kinematics of a Rigid Body (Part 1)

1. Classification of Rigid Body Planar Motion (Section 16.1)

A rigid body is an idealized system of particles where all inter-particle distances remain strictly invariant. Planar motion occurs when all particles move along paths equidistant from a fixed reference plane.

Motion Category Kinematic Definition Governing Mathematical Constraint
1. Translation Every straight line in the body remains parallel to its original orientation at all times.
  • Rectilinear: Paths of all points are parallel straight lines.
  • Curvilinear: Paths are congruent curved lines.
 rB = rA + rB/A
vB = vA (since drB/A/dt = 0)
aB = aA,   ω = 0, α = 0
2. Rotation About a Fixed Axis All particles move along concentric circular paths centered along a stationary axis of rotation. ω = θ̇ = dθ/dt
α = ω̇ = dω/dt = ω (dω/dθ)
3. General Plane Motion Simultaneous combination of translation of a reference base point and rotation about that point. vB = vA + vB/A
aB = aA + aB/A

2. Rotation About a Fixed Axis: Analytical & Vector Formulas (Sections 16.2 – 16.3)

For any point P on a rigid body located at distance r from the fixed rotation axis:

Scalar Path Relations:
• Velocity (purely tangent): v = ω r
• Tangential acceleration: at = α r = (dv/dt) (measures rate of change of speed)
• Normal acceleration: an = ω2 r = v2 / r (centripetal, points toward rotation axis)
• Total acceleration magnitude: a = √(at2 + an2) = r √(α2 + ω4)
Vector Formulation (3D Vector Cross-Products in 2D Plane):
Let ω = ω k and α = α k: $$\mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}$$ $$\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a}_t + \mathbf{a}_n = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}) = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} - \omega^2 \mathbf{r}$$

Constant Angular Acceleration (α = αc):

  1. ω(t) = ω0 + αc t
  2. θ(t) = θ0 + ω0 t + ½ αc t2
  3. ω2 = ω02 + 2αc (θ − θ0)

CHAPTER 16 — Planar Kinematics of a Rigid Body (Part 2)

3. Relative Motion Analysis: Velocity (Section 16.5)

The velocity of any point B on a rigid body is expressed relative to a reference base point A:

vB = vA + vB/A = vA + (ω × rB/A)

where vA is the absolute translation velocity of point A, and vB/A is the velocity of B observed from A (due strictly to rotation about A with angular velocity ω).

4. Instantaneous Center of Zero Velocity (IC / ICZV) (Section 16.6)

At any given instant, the planar motion of a rigid body can be modeled as pure rotation about an axis perpendicular to the plane of motion, passing through a point having zero velocity, known as the Instantaneous Center (IC).

Three Geometric Rules to Locate the IC:
  1. Given velocity direction of two points (non-parallel): Draw lines perpendicular to vA and vB. The intersection point is the IC.
    vA = ω · rA/IC &implies; ω = vA / rA/IC = vB / rB/IC
  2. Given two parallel velocities with unequal magnitudes: Draw perpendiculars across their origins and connect the tips of the velocity vectors. The intersection of these lines is the IC.
  3. Pure rolling without slipping: The physical point of contact with the stationary ground is instantaneously at rest; hence the contact point is the IC.
    vcenter = ω R,   vtop = ω (2R) = 2 vcenter.

5. Relative Motion Analysis: Acceleration (Section 16.7)

Differentiating the relative velocity equation gives the acceleration of point B relative to point A:

aB = aA + aB/A = aA + (aB/A)t + (aB/A)n
$$\mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A + \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} - \omega^2 \mathbf{r}_{B/A}$$
  • aA: Absolute acceleration of reference base point A.
  • (aB/A)t = α × rB/A (magnitude: α · rB/A, perpendicular to line AB).
  • (aB/A)n = −ω2 rB/A (magnitude: ω2 · rB/A, points directly from B toward A).

Rolling Wheel Acceleration Kinematics (Radius R, No Slip):

  • Center of mass: aG = α R (purely horizontal).
  • Contact point C: (aC)t = 0, but (aC)n = ω2 R (directed upward toward wheel center). Note: The IC has non-zero acceleration!

6. Rotating Reference Frames & Coriolis Acceleration (Section 16.8)

When analyzing a slider moving along a rotating guide arm (frame x-y-z rotating with angular velocity Ω and angular acceleration Ω̇):

$$\mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} + (\mathbf{v}_{B/A})_{xyz}$$ $$\mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A + \dot{\boldsymbol{\Omega}} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A}) + 2\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\mathbf{v}_{B/A})_{xyz} + (\mathbf{a}_{B/A})_{xyz}$$

where acor = 2Ω × (vB/A)xyz is the Coriolis Acceleration.

Exercise 16.1: Slider-Crank Kinematic Velocity & Acceleration Analysis

Problem: In a slider-crank mechanism, crank AB (length r = 0.2 m) rotates counterclockwise at constant ωAB = 10 rad/s (αAB = 0). Connecting rod BC has length L = 0.5 m. At the instant θ = 90°, crank AB is vertical, and slider C is on the horizontal centerline. Determine (a) the angular velocity ωBC, (b) the velocity of slider C, and (c) the angular acceleration αBC.

Solution:

  1. Geometry at θ = 90°:
    Point A is at (0,0), Point B is at (0, 0.2 m). Slider C is at (xC, 0).
    xC = √(L2 − r2) = √(0.52 − 0.22) = √(0.25 − 0.04) = √0.21 = 0.4583 m.
    Position vector: rC/B = 0.4583 i − 0.2 j.
  2. Velocity of crank pin B:
    vB = ωAB × rB/A = (10 k) × (0.2 j) = −2.0 i m/s.
  3. Velocity analysis of rod BC:
    vC = vB + ωBC × rC/B
    Slider C moves horizontally &implies; vC = vC i.
    vC i = −2.0 i + (ωBC k) × (0.4583 i − 0.2 j)
    vC i = −2.0 i + 0.4583 ωBC j + 0.2 ωBC i = (−2.0 + 0.2 ωBC) i + (0.4583 ωBC) j
    Equate j-components: 0.4583 ωBC = 0 &implies; ωBC = 0 rad/s.
    Equate i-components: vC = −2.0 + 0.2(0) &implies; vC = −2.00 m/s = 2.00 m/s (←).
  4. Acceleration analysis of rod BC:
    aB = αAB × rB/A − ωAB2 rB/A = 0 − (102)(0.2 j) = −20.0 j m/s2.
    aC = aB + αBC × rC/B − ωBC2 rC/B (with ωBC = 0):
    aC i = −20.0 j + (αBC k) × (0.4583 i − 0.2 j) − 0
    aC i = −20.0 j + 0.4583 αBC j + 0.2 αBC i = (0.2 αBC) i + (−20.0 + 0.4583 αBC) j
    Equate j-components: −20.0 + 0.4583 αBC = 0 &implies; αBC = 20.0 / 0.4583 = +43.64 rad/s2 (CCW).
    Equate i-components: aC = 0.2(43.64) = +8.73 m/s2 (→).
Exercise 16.2: Rolling Wheel Acceleration Kinematics

Problem: A cylinder of radius R = 0.3 m rolls without slipping along a horizontal floor. At the instant shown, its center G has velocity vG = 3 m/s (→) and acceleration aG = 1.5 m/s2 (→). Find the absolute acceleration vectors of (a) the top point A, and (b) the ground contact point C.

Solution:

  1. Angular velocity & acceleration: ω = vG / R = 3 / 0.3 = 10 rad/s (CW &implies; −10 k);   α = aG / R = 1.5 / 0.3 = 5 rad/s2 (CW &implies; −5 k).
  2. Acceleration of Top Point A: rA/G = +0.3 j.
    aA = aG + α × rA/G − ω2 rA/G = 1.5 i + (−5 k) × (0.3 j) − (102)(0.3 j)
    aA = 1.5 i + 1.5 i − 30.0 j = (3.0 i − 30.0 j) m/s2,   |aA| = √(32 + 302) = 30.15 m/s2.
  3. Acceleration of Contact Point C: rC/G = −0.3 j.
    aC = 1.5 i + (−5 k) × (−0.3 j) − (102)(−0.3 j) = 1.5 i − 1.5 i + 30.0 j = +30.0 j m/s2 (↑).

CHAPTER 17 — Planar Kinetics of a Rigid Body: Force & Acceleration (Part 1)

1. Mass Moment of Inertia (Section 17.1)

The mass moment of inertia I is a measure of a body's resistance to rotational acceleration, defined as: I = ∫ r2 dm = ∫ r2 ρ dV

The Parallel-Axis Theorem:
Allows shifting the moment of inertia from the center of mass G to any parallel axis passing through point P at a distance d:
IP = IG + m d2

• Radius of Gyration (k): A geometric parameter defined by I = m k2 &implies; k = √(I / m).
• Parallel-axis theorem using radius of gyration: kP2 = kG2 + d2.

Standard Mass Moments of Inertia for Common Homogeneous Bodies:

Geometric Shape Mass Moment of Inertia about Center of Mass (\(I_G\)) Parallel Axis Formula
Thin Slender Rod (Length L) IG = (1/12) m L2 About end pin: Iend = (1/3) m L2
Thin Circular Disk / Solid Cylinder (Radius R) IG = ½ m R2 About rim: Irim = (3/2) m R2
Circular Ring / Thin Hoop (Radius R) IG = m R2 About outer rim: Irim = 2 m R2
Thin Rectangular Plate (Sides a, b) IG = (1/12) m (a2 + b2) About edge a: I = (1/12) m a2 + (1/3) m b2
Solid Sphere (Radius R) IG = (2/5) m R2 About tangent surface: I = (7/5) m R2

2. Planar Equations of Motion: FBD = KD (Sections 17.2 – 17.3)

For any rigid body undergoing general plane motion in the x-y plane:

$$\sum F_x = m (a_G)_x$$ $$\sum F_y = m (a_G)_y$$ $$\sum M_G = I_G \alpha$$

Alternative Moment Equations about Arbitrary Point P:
Summing moments about any point P other than the mass center G introduces an inertia moment:

∑ MP = IG α + m (aG)x dy ± m (aG)y dx = IG α + [ rG/P × m aG ]k

Exercise 17.1: Physical Pendulum / Slender Rod Released from Rest

Problem: A uniform slender rod AB of mass m = 6 kg and length L = 1.2 m is pinned at end A. It is released from rest in the horizontal position (θ = 0°). At the instant of release, determine (a) the angular acceleration α of the rod, and (b) the horizontal and vertical reaction forces at pin A.

Solution:

  1. Calculate Moment of Inertia about Pin A: IA = (1/3) m L2 = (1/3)(6 kg)(1.2 m)2 = (1/3)(6)(1.44) = 2.88 kg·m2.
  2. Equation of Rotational Motion about Pin A: At release (θ = 0°), weight W = mg = (6)(9.81) = 58.86 N acts downward at L/2 = 0.6 m from A.
    ∑ MA = IA α &implies; W (L/2) = IA α
    (58.86 N)(0.6 m) = 2.88 α &implies; 35.316 = 2.88 α &implies; α = 12.26 rad/s2 (Clockwise).
  3. Acceleration of Mass Center G: Distance rG = L/2 = 0.6 m. Since released from rest, ω = 0.
    (aG)t = α rG = (12.26)(0.6) = 7.358 m/s2 (↓);   (aG)n = ω2 rG = 0.
  4. Reactions at Pin A: ∑ Fx = m (aG)n = 0 &implies; Ax = 0 N.
    ∑ Fy = m (aG)t &implies; W − Ay = m (aG)t &implies; 58.86 − Ay = (6)(7.358) = 44.15 N
    Ay = 58.86 − 44.15 = 14.71 N (↑) (Notice Ay = W/4).

Exercise 17.2: Unbalanced Pipe Rolling Down Incline with Slip Verification

Problem: A uniform solid cylinder of mass m = 20 kg and radius R = 0.2 m is released from rest on an incline of θ = 30°. The coefficients of friction are μs = 0.25 and μk = 0.20. (a) Assuming no slip, find the acceleration aG and the required friction force Ff. (b) Verify whether the cylinder slips.

Solution:

  1. Free-Body and Kinetic Diagrams: Axes: x down incline, y perpendicular to incline.
    Moment of inertia: IG = ½ m R2 = ½(20)(0.22) = 0.40 kg·m2.
  2. Equations of Motion:
    ∑ Fy = 0 &implies; N − mg cos 30° = 0 &implies; N = (20)(9.81)(0.8660) = 169.91 N.
    ∑ Fx = m aG &implies; mg sin 30° − Ff = m aG &implies; (20)(9.81)(0.5) − Ff = 20 aG &implies; 98.1 − Ff = 20 aG  (Eq. 1)
    ∑ MG = IG α &implies; Ff R = (½ m R2) α &implies; Ff = ½ m (R α)  (Eq. 2)
  3. Kinematic No-Slip Condition:
    Substitute aG = R α into Eq. 2: Ff = ½ m aG = ½(20) aG = 10 aG.
    Substitute into Eq. 1: 98.1 − 10 aG = 20 aG &implies; 30 aG = 98.1 &implies; aG = 3.27 m/s2.
    Angular acceleration: α = aG / R = 3.27 / 0.2 = 16.35 rad/s2.
    Friction required: Ff = 10(3.27) = 32.70 N.
  4. Friction Slip Check:
    Maximum available static friction: (Ff)max = μs N = (0.25)(169.91 N) = 42.48 N.
    Since Ff (32.70 N) ≤ (Ff)max (42.48 N), the cylinder rolls WITHOUT slipping.
Exercise 17.3: Slender Bar Supported by Smooth Floor and Wall

Problem: A uniform 15 kg bar AB of length L = 2 m rests against a frictionless vertical wall and floor at θ = 45°. If released from rest, find the initial angular acceleration α.

Solution:

  1. Kinematics: rG = (L/2 cosθ) i + (L/2 sinθ) j. Differentiating twice at rest (ω = 0): (aG)x = −(L/2 sinθ) α,   (aG)y = −(L/2 cosθ) α.
  2. Equations: ∑ Fx = Nwall = m [−(L/2 sin 45°) α];   ∑ Fy = Nfloor − mg = m [−(L/2 cos 45°) α].
  3. Summing moments about G: ∑ MG = IG α &implies; Nfloor (L/2 cos 45°) − Nwall (L/2 sin 45°) = (1/12 m L2) α.
  4. Substituting normal forces yields: mg (L/2 cos 45°) = [ 1/12 m L2 + m (L/2)2 ] α = (1/3 m L2) α.
    α = [ mg (L/2) cos 45° ] / [ (1/3) m L2 ] = (3 g cos 45°) / (2 L) = [ 3(9.81)(0.7071) ] / [ 2(2.0) ] = 5.20 rad/s2.

CHAPTER 18 — Planar Kinetics of a Rigid Body: Work & Energy (Part 1)

1. Kinetic Energy of a Rigid Body (Section 18.1)

The total kinetic energy of a rigid body is the sum of translational kinetic energy of its mass center G and rotational kinetic energy about G:

T = ½ m vG2 + ½ IG ω2

Specific Motion Formulations:

  • 1. Pure Translation: ω = 0 &implies; T = ½ m vG2
  • 2. Fixed-Axis Rotation (Pin at O): Using parallel-axis theorem IO = IG + m rG2 and vG = ω rG:
    T = ½ m (ω rG)2 + ½ IG ω2 = ½ (IG + m rG2) ω2 &implies; T = ½ IO ω2
  • 3. Pure Rolling without Slipping (IC at contact point C):
    T = ½ IIC ω2 = ½ (IG + m R2) ω2

2. Work of Forces and Couple Moments (Sections 18.2 – 18.3)

Action Type Work Formula Conditions
Force F UF = ∫ (F cosθ) ds = ∫ F · dr Displacement of point of application.
Couple Moment M UM = ∫ M dθ (For constant M: UM = M Δθ) Positive if M and Δθ are in same angular sense.
Weight W UW = −W ΔyG = −mg (yG2 − yG1) Depends only on vertical shift of G.
Linear Spring Us = −½ k (s22 − s12) s = elongation/compression from unextended length.
Rolling Friction (No Slip) Uf = 0 Contact point C has zero instantaneous velocity.

3. Principle of Work and Energy for a Rigid Body (Section 18.4)

T1 + ∑ U1→2 = T2

where T1 and T2 are initial and final kinetic energies, and ∑ U1→2 includes work of all external forces and couple moments.

CHAPTER 18 — Planar Kinetics of a Rigid Body: Work & Energy (Part 2)

4. Conservation of Mechanical Energy (Section 18.5)

When all work is performed exclusively by conservative forces (gravity and elastic springs):

T1 + V1 = T2 + V2

• Gravitational Potential Energy: Vg = ± m g yG (referenced to datum at center of gravity G).
• Elastic Potential Energy: Ve = + ½ k s2 (always positive).

Exercise 18.1: Stepped Spool Released on Incline with Hanging Weight

Problem: A stepped spool has total mass m = 12 kg, outer radius R = 0.4 m, hub radius r = 0.2 m, and radius of gyration kG = 0.25 m. It rolls without slipping down a 30° incline. A cord wrapped around the inner hub is connected over a frictionless pulley to a hanging mass mB = 5 kg. If the spool is released from rest, find its angular velocity after center G moves s = 2 m down the incline.

Solution:

  1. Calculate Moments of Inertia & Kinematic Relations:
    IG = m kG2 = (12 kg)(0.25 m)2 = 0.75 kg·m2.
    Since rolling without slip on outer radius R = 0.4 m: vG = ω R = 0.4 ω, and spool rotation angle θ = s / R = 2 / 0.4 = 5 rad.
    Hanging mass B displacement: The cord pays out as spool moves down &implies; sB = s − r θ = 2 − (0.2)(5) = 2 − 1 = 1.0 m (↑).
    Velocity of mass B: vB = vG − ω r = 0.4 ω − 0.2 ω = 0.2 ω.
  2. Formulate Initial and Final Kinetic Energies:
    Initial: T1 = 0 (released from rest).
    Final: T2 = ½ m (vG)2 + ½ IG ω2 + ½ mB (vB)2
    T2 = ½(12)(0.4 ω)2 + ½(0.75)ω2 + ½(5)(0.2 ω)2 = ½(12)(0.16)ω2 + 0.375 ω2 + ½(5)(0.04)ω2
    T2 = 0.96 ω2 + 0.375 ω2 + 0.10 ω2 = 1.435 ω2.
  3. Formulate Work of External Forces (∑ U1→2):
    Work of spool weight (moving down incline by ΔyG = s sin 30° = 2(0.5) = 1.0 m):
    Uspool = +m g ΔyG = (12 kg)(9.81 m/s2)(1.0 m) = +117.72 J.
    Work of hanging block B weight (lifted by sB = 1.0 m):
    Ublock = −mB g sB = −(5 kg)(9.81 m/s2)(1.0 m) = −49.05 J.
    Normal force and rolling static friction do zero work &implies; ∑ U1→2 = 117.72 − 49.05 = +68.67 J.
  4. Apply Principle of Work and Energy:
    T1 + ∑ U1→2 = T2 &implies; 0 + 68.67 = 1.435 ω2
    ω2 = 68.67 / 1.435 = 47.85 &implies; ω = 6.92 rad/s.

Exercise 18.2: Inverted Slender Rod with Torsional Spring

Problem: A uniform slender bar AB of mass m = 8 kg and length L = 1.5 m is pinned at A. A torsional spring at pin A has stiffness kt = 30 N·m/rad and is un-deformed when the bar is vertical (θ = 0°). If the bar is given a slight nudge and rotates downward, find its angular velocity when θ = 90° (horizontal).

Solution:

  1. Energy Formulation:
    Moment of inertia: IA = (1/3) m L2 = (1/3)(8)(1.52) = 6.0 kg·m2.
    State 1 (at θ1 = 0): T1 = 0, Ve1 = 0. Datum at pin A &implies; Vg1 = +mg (L/2) = (8)(9.81)(0.75) = +58.86 J.
    State 2 (at θ2 = π/2 rad): T2 = ½ IA ω2 = ½(6.0)ω2 = 3.0 ω2.
    Vg2 = 0 (bar is horizontal through datum).
    Torsional spring potential energy: Ve2 = ½ kt θ22 = ½(30)(π/2)2 = 15(2.4674) = 37.01 J.
  2. Apply Conservation of Energy:
    T1 + Vg1 + Ve1 = T2 + Vg2 + Ve2
    0 + 58.86 + 0 = 3.0 ω2 + 0 + 37.01 &implies; 3.0 ω2 = 21.85 &implies; ω2 = 7.283
    ω = 2.70 rad/s.
Exercise 18.3: General Plane Motion with Linear Spring

Problem: A 10 kg uniform disk of radius R = 0.3 m rolls without slipping. A horizontal spring (k = 100 N/m) is attached to its center G. The disk is pulled until the spring is stretched s = 0.5 m and released from rest. Find the velocity vG when the spring returns to its unstretched length.

  3. Conservation of energy: 0 + 12.5 = 7.5 vG2 + 0 &implies; vG2 = 1.667 &implies; vG = 1.29 m/s.

CHAPTER 19 — Planar Kinetics of a Rigid Body: Impulse & Momentum (Part 1)

1. Linear and Angular Momentum of a Rigid Body (Section 19.1)

Linear Momentum Vector:
L = m vG

Angular Momentum:

  • About Center of Mass G: HG = IG ω k
  • About Fixed Rotation Axis (Pin O): HO = IO ω k = (IG ω + m vG rG) k
  • About Arbitrary Point P (General Plane Motion):
    HP = IG ω k + [ rG/P × m vG ]

2. Principle of Impulse and Momentum (Sections 19.2 – 19.3)

Integrating the equations of motion over time interval t1 → t2:

Linear Impulse-Momentum:
m (vG)1 + ∑ ∫t1t2 F dt = m (vG)2

Angular Impulse-Momentum about G:
IG ω1 + ∑ ∫t1t2 MG dt = IG ω2

Angular Impulse-Momentum about Fixed Pin O:
IO ω1 + ∑ ∫t1t2 MO dt = IO ω2

3. Conservation of Angular and Linear Momentum (Section 19.3)

  • If external impulse ∑ ∫ F dt = 0 &implies; m(vG)1 = m(vG)2.
  • If external moment impulse about a fixed point O is zero:
    ∑ ∫ MO dt = 0 &implies; (HO)1 = (HO)2

CHAPTER 19 — Planar Kinetics of a Rigid Body: Impulse & Momentum (Part 2)

4. Eccentric Impact of Rigid Bodies (Section 19.4)

An impact is eccentric when the line of collision impact does not pass through the mass centers of the colliding bodies. This induces sudden changes in both linear velocity and angular velocity.

Coefficient of Restitution (e) along Line of Impact (n-axis):
e = [ (vB)n2 − (vA)n2 ] / [ (vA)n1 − (vB)n1 ]

where (vA)n and (vB)n are the normal velocities of the exact contact points immediately before and after the collision.

Exercise 19.1: Ballistic Impact on a Suspended Slender Rod

Problem: A uniform slender bar OA of mass M = 4 kg and length L = 0.9 m hangs vertically from smooth pin O. A bullet of mass m = 0.02 kg is fired horizontally with speed v0 = 400 m/s and embeds itself into the lower tip A. Find (a) the angular velocity ω of the bar immediately after impact, and (b) the impulsive reaction at pin O.

Solution:

  1. Conservation of Angular Momentum about Pin O:
    During the microsecond collision, external pin reactions exert zero moment about pin O &implies; (HO)1 = (HO)2.
    Initial angular momentum: (HO)1 = m v0 L = (0.02 kg)(400 m/s)(0.9 m) = 7.20 kg·m2/s.
    Final moment of inertia about O: IO = (1/3) M L2 + m L2 = (1/3)(4)(0.92) + (0.02)(0.92) = 1.08 + 0.0162 = 1.0962 kg·m2.
    Final angular momentum: (HO)2 = IO ω.
    7.20 = 1.0962 ω &implies; ω = 6.57 rad/s.
  2. Impulsive Reaction at Pin O (∫ Rx dt):
    Linear impulse-momentum in horizontal x-direction:
    m v0 + ∫ Rx dt = M (vG)2 + m (vA)2
    where (vG)2 = ω (L/2) = (6.57)(0.45) = 2.956 m/s, and (vA)2 = ω L = (6.57)(0.9) = 5.913 m/s.
    (0.02)(400) + ∫ Rx dt = (4)(2.956) + (0.02)(5.913) &implies; 8.0 + ∫ Rx dt = 11.824 + 0.118 = 11.942
    ∫ Rx dt = 11.942 − 8.00 = +3.94 N·s (→).

Exercise 19.2: Bowling Ball Transition from Slipping to Pure Rolling

Problem: A bowling ball (solid sphere, m = 7 kg, R = 0.11 m, IG = (2/5)m R2) is thrown onto a lane with horizontal velocity v0 = 8 m/s and zero initial spin (ω0 = 0). Kinetic friction coefficient is μk = 0.15. Determine (a) the time t1 required for the ball to stop slipping and begin pure rolling, and (b) its final velocity v1.

Solution:

  1. Normal Force and Kinetic Friction:
    ∑ Fy = 0 &implies; N = mg = (7)(9.81) = 68.67 N.
    Friction force opposing slipping: Fk = μk N = (0.15)(68.67) = 10.30 N (←).
  2. Linear and Angular Impulse Equations:
    Linear momentum: m v0 − Fk t = m v(t) &implies; 8 − (10.30/7) t = v(t) &implies; v(t) = 8 − 1.471 t.
    Angular momentum about G: 0 + (Fk R) t = IG ω(t) &implies; (10.30 × 0.11) t = [ (2/5)(7)(0.112) ] ω(t)
    1.133 t = 0.03388 ω(t) &implies; ω(t) = 33.44 t.
  3. Kinematic Pure Rolling Condition at t1 (v1 = ω1 R):
    8 − 1.471 t1 = (33.44 t1)(0.11) = 3.678 t1
    8 = (1.471 + 3.678) t1 = 5.149 t1 &implies; t1 = 1.55 s.
  4. Final Rolling Velocity:
    v1 = 8 − 1.471(1.55) = 5.72 m/s.
Exercise 19.3: Conservation of Angular Momentum (Reeling Cable)

Problem: A 10 kg disk with radius R = 0.5 m is spinning at ω1 = 20 rad/s about a central shaft. An identical stationary disk is dropped onto it. Friction causes them to reach a common angular speed ω2 without external torque. Find ω2 and the percentage energy lost.

Solution:

  1. Conservation of Angular Momentum: I ω1 + 0 = (I + I) ω2 &implies; ω2 = ω1 / 2 = 10.0 rad/s.
  2. Energy before: T1 = ½ I ω12. Energy after: T2 = ½ (2I) (ω1/2)2 = (1/4) I ω12 = ½ T1. 50% energy lost to friction heat.

CHAPTER 20 — Three-Dimensional Kinematics of a Rigid Body (Part 1)

1. Rotation About a Fixed Point (Section 20.1)

When a rigid body is constrained to rotate about a single stationary fixed point O, its instantaneous angular velocity is a 3D vector ω = ωx i + ωy j + ωz k.

Velocity and Acceleration Vectors of Point P: $$\mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}$$ $$\mathbf{a} = \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}) = \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v}$$

where α = ω̇ is the 3D angular acceleration vector.

2. Time Derivative of a Vector Measured from Fixed vs. Rotating Frames (Section 20.2)

Let XYZ be a fixed inertial reference frame, and xyz be a reference frame rotating with angular velocity Ω:

(dA/dt)XYZ = (dA/dt)xyz + Ω × A

Angular Acceleration Derivative: If frame xyz is attached to the body so Ω = ωbody:

α = (dω/dt)XYZ = (dω/dt)xyz + ω × ω = (ω̇)xyz + 0 = (ω̇)xyz

3. General 3D Motion (Section 20.3)

Combines 3D translation of base point A and 3D rotation about A:

$$\mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A}$$ $$\mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A + \boldsymbol{\alpha} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A})$$

4. 3D Rotating & Translating Reference Frames (Section 20.4)

$$\mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} + (\mathbf{v}_{B/A})_{xyz}$$ $$\mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A + \dot{\boldsymbol{\Omega}} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A} + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A}) + 2\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\mathbf{v}_{B/A})_{xyz} + (\mathbf{a}_{B/A})_{xyz}$$

Exercise 20.1: Gyroscopic Precession & 3D Acceleration of a Spinning Disk

Problem: A horizontal shaft OA of length L = 0.8 m precesses about the vertical Z-axis at constant ω1 = 2 rad/s. Mounted at A is a disk of radius R = 0.3 m spinning about shaft OA at constant relative spin ω2 = 25 rad/s. Determine (a) the total angular velocity ω, (b) total angular acceleration α, and (c) the velocity and acceleration of the highest point P on the disk at this instant.

Solution:

  1. Coordinate Systems:
    Let rotating frame xyz have origin at O, with x along shaft OA, z vertical, and y completing right-hand rule.
    Precession vector: Ω = ω1 = 2 k rad/s. Relative spin: ωrel = 25 i rad/s.
  2. Total Angular Velocity and Angular Acceleration:
    ω = ωrel + Ω = (25 i + 2 k) rad/s.
    α = dω/dt = (ω̇)xyz + Ω × ω = 0 + (2 k) × (25 i + 2 k) = +50 j rad/s2.
  3. Position Vector of Highest Point P:
    rP = L i + R z = (0.8 i + 0.3 k) m.
  4. Velocity of Point P:
    vP = ω × rP = (25 i + 2 k) × (0.8 i + 0.3 k)
    vP = (25)(0.3)(i × k) + (2)(0.8)(k × i) = −7.5 j + 1.6 j = −5.90 j m/s.
  5. Acceleration of Point P:
    aP = α × rP + ω × vP
    α × rP = (50 j) × (0.8 i + 0.3 k) = −40 k + 15 i = 15 i − 40 k.
    ω × vP = (25 i + 2 k) × (−5.9 j) = −147.5 k + 11.8 i = 11.8 i − 147.5 k.
    aP = (15 + 11.8) i + (−40 − 147.5) k = (26.8 i − 187.5 k) m/s2,   |aP| = 189.4 m/s2.
Exercise 20.2: Rolling Cone on a Flat Plane

Problem: A cone (base radius R = 0.2 m, height H = 0.6 m) rolls without slipping on a horizontal floor such that its apex remains fixed at O. The center of the base precesses at ω1 = 3 rad/s. Find the total angular velocity ω.

Solution:

  1. Semi-vertex angle: tan γ = R / H = 0.2 / 0.6 = 1/3 &implies; γ = 18.43°.
  2. No slip on contact line: The instantaneous axis of zero velocity is the contact generator line.
    ω = ω1 / sin γ = 3 / sin(18.43°) = 3 / 0.3162 = 9.49 rad/s.
